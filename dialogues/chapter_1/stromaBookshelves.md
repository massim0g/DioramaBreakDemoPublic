>Physics
>	![[]]
>	Hi dee hi kids! Welcome to the wonderful world of **physics**!
>	What's that? Great question!
>	**Physics** is the study of **objects** and **forces**! What they're made of, how they move, and how they interact with each other!
>	Here are some examples of physics facts you may or may not have known:
>	- An object at rest will stay at rest. An object in motion will stay in motion.
>	- Objects which cannot be broken down into other objects are known as **components**. Objects which can are known as **composites**.
>	- When an object is subjected to enough force within a short enough period, it takes **damage**. 
>	- If an object takes enough damage, it will be **transformed** or, more often, **destroyed** completely.
>	- All objects have an innate capacity to retain and conduct Air. Force enough Air through an object beyond this capacity and it will take damage.
>	- When objects are disturbed or destroyed, they can produce intangible **detritus particles**.
>Pneumatics
>	![[]]
>	Air is one of the fundamental forces of nature, alongside the kinetic force. It can be split into both **imbued** and **free** air.
>	Free Air can be described as a vector field assigning any arbitrary region of space a "density" value and flow direction.
>	Air will generally flow from regions of high density to low density.
>	In addition, free Air may flow into and out of objects. We call Air within an object imbued Air.
>	At times, the flow of Air to or from either state may exceed an object's innate capacity to support it. 
>	The excess flow in these cases is converted to both kinetic force and luminance.
>	The exact behavior of Air flow can be described by the following... 
>Face Science
>	![[]]
>	As best we can tell, the observable universe is contained entirely within a cuboid with a width and length of approximately 70 kilometers.
>	Its height is at present unknown. Conditions both far above and below sea level prove too inhospitable for even our probes.
>	Regardless, we can find at the horizontal limits of this cube a luminous border. 
>	Any attempts to damage, pierce, or chip at it in any way have proven fruitless.
>	It is unclear, even, if this is "matter" as commonly understood, or simply the very edge of space itself.
>	But these are questions for astronomers. Let us move now to the *contents* of this cuboid.
>	First, a layer of earth of, once again, unknown depth.
>	We have explored down to at least 4km, but logistical and environmental obstacles make going any further untenable, even in the modern day.
>	Second, a layer of water, forming our oceans. 
>	Their average depth is estimated to be around 60 meters, and the deepest observed point has been measured at 163 meters below sea level.
>	Thirdly, the continent of Face itself. A set of islands rising above the sea.
>	Its name, as is no doubt obvious, comes from its distinctive shape.
>	For what purpose our home is shaped so, none can say for sure.   
>	Finally, then, our atmosphere. 
>	Air seems to seep upwards from below, densely filling the space at sea level and rapidly dissipating as it spreads upwards through space.
>	Even at sea level, the spread is not even. Fixtures and other "natural" fonts have been discovered emitting at noticeably higher rates than background.
>	It is theorized that such fonts may in truth be the sole source of terrestrial Air, existing in greater numbers and potency at higher depths.
>Manufacturing
>	![[]]
>	In normal everyday life, we think of things as solid, fixed.
>	But how, then, do we bend the materials of our world into all the marvelous tools of modern life?
>	The answer is **shaping**!
>	With a skilled hand, objects may be bent, twisted, holed, fused together, and generally formed then assembled into anything we might need.
>	Now, materials soft enough to do this manually are few and far between.
>	But imbue them with enough Air, and many materials achieve surprising plasticity!
>	Combine with machine hands, and machine strength, and the possibilities become limitless.
>	It's quite remarkable what can be done with just the simple manipulation and transference of Force and Air!

## poliSciSelect

>The Stromal State
>	![[]]
>	The Stromal State is a city-state comprising of the Stromal capital, located on the West Eye, and various outlying settlements stretching as far as Lacrima.
>	It has the world's fourth-largest population at roughly 3 million individuals, and is responsible for roughly one-seventh of world GDP. 
>	Governance of the state is handled entirely by The Ministry of Higher Affairs (commonly "The Ministry").
>	It is not incorrect to say that their organizational structure is effectively one with the state government's.
>	Originally founded to study and interpret the Original Repository, the Ministry is one of the oldest institutions in the world.
>	It uses a hierarchical system of top-down governance, with three major "ranks" for officials.
>	Each rank selects applicants from the rank below to promote into available openings. Officials typically serve until death or promotion.
>	Secretaries, the lowest ranked officials, each manage a "hall" of 50-200 members of the general population. There exist roughly 24,000 secretaries.
>	Executives typically manage larger neighborhoods and are responsible for local policy implementation. At this time there are 192 total executives.
>	Finally, the high council, comprising at all times of 5 members, decides on all policy for the state.
>	While the state does not usually see a single mayor in office, a "war mayor" may be elected by the high council to serve in wartime, with strict term limits.
>	The nation holds a Modeler that allows them to shape plant life into useful edifices.
>	This has allowed them to construct an extremely dense vertical city against the western Great Tree and in the forest below.
>	Culturally, the stromal society is one that strongly values rule of law, stability, and a harmonious living with their concept of the world's destined path. 
>	The Ministry maintains unusually strong diplomatic ties with Haguki. Citizens of both nations enjoy extensive mutual free trade and movement rights.
>The Haguki Confederation
>	![[]]
>	The Haguki Confederation is a loose collection of settlements and cities spread throughout the Haguki region.
>	It has the world's third-largest population at roughly 6.3 million individuals, and is responsible for roughly one-seventh of world GDP.
>	While each municipality is ostensibly part of this federated whole, each maintains its own borders and is effectively self-governing.
>	The state exists essentially as a strong mutual defense and non-aggression pact, with the exception of the nation's characteristic **meadow wars**.
>	Meadow wars, as a practice, have existed in the region for hundreds of years.
>	Historically, participants would agree to a preset date, location, and stakes. Their respective armies would be fielded, and then fight to exhaustion.
>	Over time, these "wars" have grown increasingly formalized.
>	With the increasing urbanization of Hagukese territory, they now often take place in controlled stadiums.
>	A complicated code of honor governs these conflicts and determines the sorts of tactics and weapons used.
>	Serious violators risk retaliation from all Hagukese polities. 
>	While deaths in these battles are inevitable, they are frowned upon and tend to see compensation from the victor.
>	At all times, a shogun mayor holds office. They handle foreign relations and may implement small changes to common law.
>	This title is included amongst the many things that may be staked in a meadow war.
>	Culturally, as might be apparent, the Hagukese are a strong honor culture that values excellence in what they deem "the great arts", particularly warfare.
>	The confederation holds a Modeler which can "compress" foodstuffs into useful construction material.
>	It is considered by many to be the least valuable one, as it offers little advantage over traditional production methods.
>The Central Government
>	![[]]
>	The Central Government is a democratic city-state whose territory comprises the highly urbanized Tongue region and surrounding districts.
>	It has the world's largest population at 12 million, and is responsible for about a third of world GDP.
>	The Central Government is a parliamentary representative democracy. The population elects diet members via a nation-wide proportional voting system.
>	The mayor is also elected directly, and serves as head of state and commander-in-chief of the military.
>	Elections are held every 4 years, though as of recently they may be postponed indefinitely in times of crisis.
>	It is difficult to point out values held universally in Tongue's diverse culture.
>	However, a general appreciation of industriousness, wealth, and self-reliance is often apparent.
>	The nation holds a Modeler which can mass-produce certain "template" shapes out of any material.
>	Many of the Tongue region's characteristic skyscrapers are made affordably through use of these easily assembled parts.
>	Occupying the site of humanity's origin, the CG considers itself the "one and only true government of Face" and has named itself as such.
>	The insistence of the leadership and citizenry on this point has caused no shortage of friction in the past.
>Front Academy and the Nez region
>	![[]]
>	It is difficult to categorize anything in the Nez region as a "state" in the traditional sense.
>	However, as ostensibly the largest entity on the island, Front Academy is typically seen as its "representative".
>	The region as a whole has a population of only 1.2 million, yet is responsible for a whopping tenth of world GDP.
>	Originally founded by a society dedicated to the advancement of the sciences, the academy has become the world's premier center for technological innovation.
>	Funded primarily through private investment\*, it now supports a network of privately-run institutions large enough to rival any nation.
>	\*including, in no small part, patent-gratuity agreements with graduate students.
>	Culturally, the region highly values autonomy and personal freedoms.
>	Nevertheless, a strong cooperative spirit exists, as well as a unified will to defend said values.
>	Thanks to the region's highly defensible geography, the need for a single centralized military force is drastically reduced.
>	In lieu of one, then, most residents of the region have voluntarily taken up arms training.
>	The Academy holds a Modeler which can be used to transport and affix earthen substances with incredible strength.
>	The castle which houses the Academy stretches impossibly high thanks to this power.

>Basics of Breath
>	![[]]
>	For living objects, absorbing Air into the body is a basic survival function.
>	We call air imbued this way **Breath**.
>	Unlike nonliving objects, living creatures have some measure of conscious and unconscious control over this process.
>	With training, skilled combatants are able to precisely expel stored breath to enhance movement, repel blows, and hit harder.
>Breath Articulation
>	![[]]
>	A combatant's breath and (by definition) their control over it may be extended to objects they are in contact with.
>	This includes clothing, tools, weapons, and even other living beings.
>	Controlling breath in this way requires deep familiarity with the object and grows significantly more difficult the larger and more "detached" they are.
>	Still, the benefits can be well worth it. Exploiting the size and capacity of nonliving things can result in incredible exertions of power.
>	Further, even simple clothing and armor, properly infused, can noticeably increase a combatant's ability to absorb damage.
>Hitting your Opponent
>	![[]]
>	All combat is ultimately about delivering force to your opponent's body.
>	While shocks below a certain threshold are of course harmless, any above this threshold will begin to deal damage.
>	The more can be delivered in a short amount of time, the more damage is dealt.
>	It is worth noting though that many prefer lighter, smaller weapons, trading force of impact for speed. One must actually land the blows, after all.
>	We also find that, to a point, sharper objects have a noticeable multiplicative effect on damage dealt for the same amount of force.
>	Consider all this carefully when selecting a weapon.
>Air Attacks
>	![[]]
>	When an object absorbs air above its capacity, it will take damage, and living bodies are no exception.
>	Many weapons, particularly ranged ones, exploit this effect, attempting to quickly deliver sharp bursts of air to briefly overload a target.
>	To defend against this, one may use armor made of material with high Air capacity or conductance, to absorb or redirect the attack.
>	Such armor tends to be comparatively vulnerable to physical force though, so tradeoffs must be considered.
>Incapacitation
>	![[]]
>	Should an object receive sufficient damage, it will be shattered or obliterated.
>	Living beings can recover from damage remarkably quickly (when nourished with food or an Air-rich environment), but they too are vulnerable to this.
>	Notably, sufficient damage to a living being will render them unconscious. 
>	Through careful battlefield observation, it has been found that the damage necessary to kill a living being is roughly twice that necessary to incapacitate them.
